# Decrypto Rules
<!-- rule-section: overview -->
## Overview

In **Decrypto**, two teams try to communicate three-digit codes to their own teammates while learning enough from public clues to intercept the opposing team's codes. Make clues clear enough for your team to map to its secret words, but vague enough that the other team cannot reconstruct that map.

The standard game uses two teams of 2–4 players (4–8 total). A 3-player variant is included at the end.
<!-- rule-section: game-objective -->
## Game Objective

In Decrypto, you are one half of a code team protecting four secret words. Your goal is to help teammates decode your numbered messages twice before the opposing team intercepts two of yours.
<!-- rule-section: components -->
## Components

- 2 team screens
- 110 Keyword cards (440 words)
- 48 Code cards
- 4 Interception tokens (white)
- 4 Miscommunication tokens (black)
- Sand timer and note sheets
<!-- rule-section: setup -->
## Setup

![Each team hides four numbered keywords behind its screen; an Encryptor turns a three-digit code into three clues](/images/rules/decrypto/screen-code.svg)

1. Split into two teams. Each team takes a screen, note sheet, and the tokens for its side.
2. Randomly draw one Keyword card for each team and slide it behind that team's screen. Its four words sit in pockets **1–4**. Everyone on that team can see the words; opponents cannot.
3. Shuffle the Code cards and place them within reach. Keep the Interception and Miscommunication tokens visible.
4. Choose an **Encryptor** for the first round on each team. The role should rotate between teammates from round to round.
<!-- rule-section: giving-a-code -->
## Giving a Code

Each round, each team's Encryptor secretly draws a Code card with a sequence of three different digits, such as **4–2–1**. They give **three clues in that order**, one clue for each of their team's hidden numbered words.

For example, if a team has `1 pig`, `2 candy`, `3 tent`, `4 son`, an Encryptor with code 4–2–1 might say: **Sam — striped — pink**.

Clues must relate to the meaning of the team's words. They may be one word or a phrase, but cannot directly say, translate, spell, or otherwise trivially reveal a secret word. Record every clue on the note sheet: old clues are the opponents' evidence.
<!-- rule-ui: tabs -->
<!-- rule-section: round-order -->
## Round Order

![From round two onward, opponents try to intercept first; then the owning team decodes its own clues](/images/rules/decrypto/intercept-order.svg)
<!-- rule-section: round-1 -->
### Round 1

There is no interception attempt. Each team hears its Encryptor's three clues, discusses, and guesses its own three-digit code. A wrong own-code guess gives that team **1 Miscommunication token**.
<!-- rule-section: round-2-and-later -->
### Round 2 and later

For each team's transmission, resolve in this order:

1. **Intercept:** the opposing team studies all recorded clues for that screen and secretly guesses the new three-digit code.
2. **Own decode:** the owning team guesses the code from its Encryptor's clues.
3. Reveal both guesses and award any tokens.

An interception guess can be right even if the owning team also decodes correctly. A correct own-code guess does not award a token; it only avoids a Miscommunication token.
<!-- rule-section: tokens-and-game-end -->
## Tokens and Game End

![Two white Interception tokens win; two black Miscommunication tokens lose; after round eight compare guessed keywords](/images/rules/decrypto/tokens-end.svg)

| Result | Token |
|---|---|
| You correctly intercept the opposing team's code | Take **1 Interception token** (white). |
| Your own team guesses its Encryptor's code incorrectly | Take **1 Miscommunication token** (black). |

The game ends immediately when a team gets its **second Interception token** (that team wins) or its **second Miscommunication token** (that team loses).

If neither condition has ended the game after Round 8, each team tries to identify the other team's four hidden keywords from the recorded clues. The team that correctly identifies more keywords wins.
<!-- rule-section: 3-player-variant -->
## 3-Player Variant

One player is the **Interceptor**; the other two players form the Encryptor team. Use the normal clue and interception procedure. The Interceptor wins by gaining 2 Interception tokens before the end of Round 5; otherwise, the Encryptor team wins.
<!-- rule-section: good-practice -->
## Good Practice

- Rotate Encryptors so every teammate reads codes and gives clues.
- Keep notes private to your team, but keep spoken clues public.
- Use a fresh Code card every round; do not reuse its digit order.
- A clue that is too obvious may save this round but teaches the opposition your keyword for later rounds.
