# Eternal Decks Rules

<!-- rule-section: overview -->

<!-- rule-section: overview -->
## Overview

**Eternal Decks** (Hiroken, 2025) is a **1–4 player cooperative card puzzle**. Each player begins with only five Number Cards. Build three Field rows under increasingly awkward restrictions, revive the Eternals at their ends, and add their eight-card decks to keep everyone alive.

The game has six Stages (A–F). This page teaches the shared system through **Stage A: Nature Star**, the recommended introduction. Later Stages change the goal and add their own components and rules.

<!-- rule-section: game-objective -->
## Game Objective

You are explorers keeping the cycle of life going. In Stage A, gain **4 Stars** before any player cannot take an action or the River produces a Game Over card. The team wins or loses together.

<!-- rule-section: components -->
## Components

- 24 Eternal cards and 128 Eternal Deck cards (16 decks of 8)
- 20 Starting Cards (four five-card player decks)
- 16 Field Cards and a shared Game Board
- 12 Jewels, 6 Stars, and 3 Heart tokens
- 8 Communication Discs (two per player color)
- River Rare Cards, Game Over Cards, Stage cards, and stage-specific material

<!-- rule-section: stage-a-setup -->
## Stage A setup

Follow the Stage A setup diagram in the rulebook for the number and placement of Eternals. For a first game, use **Beginner Mode**.

1. Put **MOUNTAIN** in the top Field row, **CAVE** in the middle, and **CAMP** at the bottom.
2. Put the Stage A Eternals at the ends of the Field rows as instructed; their decks remain separate until revived.
3. Each player takes their color's five-card Starting Deck, shuffles it, draws to a hand of **3**, and takes two matching Communication Discs.
4. Set out the Jewels, Stars, Hearts, River Rare Cards, and the Stage A card. Choose a starting player.

<!-- rule-section: your-turn -->
## Your turn

**Take 1 action → refill to 3 cards → next player clockwise.** On your turn, choose exactly one action:

1. **Play a card** — to a Field row, to the River, or use an Ability Card.
2. **Generate a Jewel** — use a new recipe, then give that Jewel to a revived Eternal.
3. **Give a card** — spend 1 Heart to give any card facedown to a teammate.

After the action, draw from your own Player Deck until you have **3 cards in hand**. Do not discard down if you already have 3 or more. If your deck is empty, draw what you can; having fewer than 3 cards is dangerous.

<!-- rule-section: playing-to-the-field -->
## Playing to the Field

![Three Field rows: Mountain ascends, Cave descends, and Camp uses only the common restrictions](/images/rules/eternal-decks/field-placement.svg)

Choose a row and place one card in its **leftmost empty space**. A Field card can never move until its row clears.

**Common restrictions for every row:** the new card cannot be next to a card with the **same color** or **same number**.

| Field row | Extra restriction |
|---|---|
| **MOUNTAIN** | Numbers must ascend, low to high. |
| **CAVE** | Numbers must descend, high to low. |
| **CAMP** | No extra restriction; use only the common restrictions. |

Two-color cards are oriented when played; their exposed left and right colors must both be legal next to their neighbors. **Rare Cards** ignore the usual Field and row-placement restrictions, unless an active Curse says rares cannot enter the Field.

<!-- rule-section: reviving-eternals-and-curses -->
## Reviving Eternals and Curses

![A legal final Field card reaches an Eternal, then its deck joins that player's deck and its Curse becomes active](/images/rules/eternal-decks/revive-eternal.svg)

Play a card into the final space of a row to **revive** an Eternal there. Only the player who completed the row gains that Eternal's **eight-card deck**: place it at the bottom of that player's Player Deck. If that deck was empty, it immediately becomes their deck.

The revived Eternal moves to the Revival Area and activates its **Curse**, which adds a restriction until covered by a Jewel. For example, the Phoenix prevents number 1 cards from being played to the Field; the King prevents number 6 cards. A cursed card may still be sent to the River or spent to generate a Jewel.

When a row clears, begin its next lap with the current Field-card rule. After a row's fourth lap, it closes and cannot receive more Field cards.

<!-- rule-section: the-river-and-ability-cards -->
## The River and Ability Cards

Cards played to the **River** ignore Field placement rules and active number/color Curses. When the fifth (or later) card enters the River:

- Take one River Rare Card into the active player's hand.
- Move all River cards to the Discard Area.
- If there is no River Rare Card left, draw a Game Over Card instead: the team immediately loses.

Most Ability Cards are played directly to the Discard Area, then resolve their printed effect. An Ability Card may instead be played into the River without using its effect.

<!-- rule-section: generate-a-jewel -->
## Generate a Jewel

You may generate a Jewel only if at least one revived Eternal does not already have one. Each recipe can be used **once per game**. Put the cards used into the River, take the matching Jewel, and place it on any eligible Eternal to cover and remove its Curse.

| Jewel recipe | Cards used |
|---|---|
| Any two / any three | 2 or 3 cards |
| Same color / different colors | 3 cards |
| 1 or 9 | 1 Number Card |
| Same number | 2 or 3 cards |
| 1 / 5 / 9 | one each of 1, 5, and 9 |

For a two-color card, choose one of its colors for a recipe. A Rare Card can be any color or number. The **1 / 5 / 9** recipe also grants a Star.

<!-- rule-section: communication -->
## Communication

![Communication Discs indicate intended spaces without revealing the Number Cards in your hand](/images/rules/eternal-decks/communication-discs.svg)

You may not directly state the **number**, **color**, or two-color status of Number Cards in your hand. You may discuss public cards, past plays, Curses, Rare Cards, Ability Cards, routes you intend to take, and the team's deck pressure.

At any time, place or move your two Communication Discs on spaces where you think you can play. They are hints, not commitments, and are the main way to coordinate without revealing hidden cards.

<!-- rule-section: stage-a-stars-and-game-end -->
## Stage A Stars and game end

There are six possible Stars in Stage A; gain any **4** to win. The sources are:

- Give Jewels to the 1st–3rd revived Eternals.
- Give Jewels to the 4th–6th revived Eternals.
- Revive all 9 Eternals.
- Complete the 1 / 5 / 9 Jewel recipe.
- Complete the fourth lap of the bottom row.
- Play all three Stage A Eternal Ability Cards.

The team loses immediately if a player cannot perform any of the three actions, or if the River has no Rare Card left when it must give a River Bonus. If victory and defeat happen during the same turn, **victory wins**.

<!-- rule-section: later-stages-and-solo-play -->
## Later Stages and solo play

Stages B–F reuse the action loop but replace the Stage A Star goal with stage-specific objectives, generally earning **four Keys**. They introduce Ghost Town, Labyrinth, Gatekeeper, Silent, and Dragon challenges. Solo play uses three Golems as separate decks; use the dedicated solo setup and story rules rather than simply running a one-player Stage A setup.
