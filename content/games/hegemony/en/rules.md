# Hegemony: Lead Your Class to Victory

<!-- rule-section: overview -->
## Overview

**Hegemony: Lead Your Class to Victory** is a 2–4 player political-economy board game. You play the Working Class, the Middle Class, the Capitalist Class, or the State: some want a better life, some want to grow their companies, and some must keep public services and the budget running. Everyone shares the same markets — raising wages, buying resources, collecting taxes, or pushing a policy forces the other classes to adapt.

<!-- rule-section: objective -->
## Game Objective

Gain **victory points (VP)** through the scoring opportunities of your own class. After the fifth round ends, resolve loans first, then apply each class's end-game scoring; **the player with the most VP wins**.

## Setup

> Pick classes by player count → place the board and policy starts → complete your own class setup list → keep your hand private.

<!-- rule-ui: sidebar default=setup-board -->
1. <!-- rule-item: setup-board -->

2. <!-- rule-item: setup-classes -->
   **Choose classes**


   2. **2 players** use the Capitalist and Working classes, **3 players** add the Middle Class, **4 players** use all four classes. Unplayed classes still take part in the economy through the official automatic setup.

   **Prepare player assets**


   3. Each player takes their player board, starting cards, money, companies, workers, influence, and Bill markers, then completes their class's setup list item by item.


4. <!-- rule-item: setup-markets -->
   **Fill markets and public areas**


   4. Set up starting companies, workers, public companies, the company market, export cards, immigration cards, events, and the political agenda for your player count. Staffing, wages, output, and prices are printed on the components.


5. <!-- rule-item: setup-first-game -->
   **First game**


   5. First time playing: read your own player aid first, then let someone who knows the game walk through round 1.

6. <!-- rule-item: starting-summary -->
   **Starting Summary**

   | **Capitalist Class** | Revenue `120`, Food `1`, Luxury `2`, Education `2`, influence `1`; sell Food for `12`, other saleable services for `8`. Take the free-trade area, 7 hand cards, 3 blue Bills, and reveal 4 companies as the market. |
   | **Middle Class (3 / 4 players)** | Population `10`, Prosperity `0`, money `40`, influence `1`, Food `1`, Health `1`; selling prices same as the Capitalist. 7 hand cards, 3 yellow Bills, a 3-company market.    Board side: Treasury starts at **120**; the voting bag holds **8** cubes of each color (include Middle cubes even in 2-player games); the tax multiplier starts at **5**; the round marker starts at 1. Public services: Health / Education `5` and Media `3` with 2 players; `6` and `4` with 3 / 4 players.

<!-- rule-section: round -->
## The Round

> **Round 1 skips Preparation.** Every later round: Preparation → Actions → Production → Elections (only if Bills exist) → Scoring.

The game lasts **five rounds**. After round 5's scoring, resolve loans first, then each class's end-game bonus; **the player with the most VP wins**.

![Each class uses its own action cards, but all actions feed one shared economy](/images/rules/hegemony/shared-economy.svg)

![Official reference for the five phases of a round](/images/rules/hegemony/official-round-structure.jpg)

| Phase | Order | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| **Preparation** | — | Advance the round; pay interest, refill hands, refill markets, add workers, or reveal events |
| **Actions** | Working → Middle → Capitalist → State | Each class present takes its **5** personal turns |
| **Production** | State → Capitalist → Middle → Working | Companies produce, households eat, check the IMF, pay taxes |
| **Elections** | Only if Bills exist | Refill the voting bag, then resolve the Bills one by one |
| **Scoring** | — | Each class scores this round; the State first applies unresolved Event penalties |

<!-- rule-ui: sidebar default=preparation-phase -->
1. <!-- rule-item: preparation-phase -->
   **Preparation**


   From round 2 onward, advance the round, pay interest, refill hands and markets, add workers, and reveal any scheduled Events. Round 1 skips this phase.

   ---


2. <!-- rule-item: action-phase -->
   **The Action Phase**


   > **Free Action (optional) → play 1 card, or discard 1 card for a Basic Action → Free Action (optional)**
   >
   > At most **1** Free Action per turn, before or after the Main Action. After your 5 turns, keep **2** cards in hand; the next Preparation refills you to 7.

   ![Your turn: optional free action, then play a card or discard for a basic action](/images/rules/hegemony/turn-action.svg)

   - Want to play a card for its effect? First check the policy requirement. If you cannot meet it, the card can only be discarded for a Basic Action.
   - A card's Legitimacy icon applies **only in 4-player games and only when you actually use the card effect**; discarding for a Basic Action never triggers it.
   - When a card tells you to take a Basic Action, the usual restrictions still apply unless the card explicitly overrides them.

   ---


3. <!-- rule-item: production-phase -->
   **The Production Phase**



4. <!-- rule-item: companies-workers -->
   **Companies and Committed Workers**


   1. Each operating company first pays the **whole company wage** printed on the card once, then takes all of its output.
   2. A regular company must fill every worker slot to produce; an **fully automatic company** produces even with no workers.
   3. A struck company normally neither produces nor pays wages that round.
   4. Wages go to the class that owns the workers; output goes to the company owner's storage.
   5. After production, all lying-down **committed workers** stand up and can be moved again next round.

   > A company pays its **1** total wage only once — not per worker.

   Public services store up to "total output of that kind of public company + 6"; private storage limits are printed on the player boards. If storage is full, the excess resources are lost.


5. <!-- rule-item: food-imf-taxes -->
   **Food, the IMF, and Taxes**


   - **Working and Middle households must eat**: each eats Food equal to its **Population**. Eat your own first, then buy, then import; if a mandatory cost cannot be paid, you must take a Loan.
   - When the State's loans exceed the Fiscal Policy limit, first try to pay `55` per loan; if that fails, the **IMF intervenes**.
   - Taxes always go to the **Treasury**. Working / Middle pay income tax; the Capitalist also pays employment and corporate tax.

   ---


6. <!-- rule-item: elections-policies -->
   **Elections and Policies**



7. <!-- rule-item: how-to-vote -->
   **How to Vote**


   Refill the voting bag: Working adds "half its Population, rounded up"; Capitalist adds "half its operating companies"; Middle adds the higher of those two measures. With 2 players there is no Middle player, but still add **5** Middle cubes. The State adds no cubes; it gains Personal Influence equal to its lowest Legitimacy.

   Each player has **3** Bill markers. To change a policy: choose a policy with no Bill on it and place your marker on the current position's **adjacent** space. Normally the Bill waits for Elections; you may instead spend `1` Influence right after proposing to resolve it immediately.

   1. Declare for or against in the order Working → Middle → Capitalist → State. A proposer must vote in favor and cannot abstain.
   2. Draw **5** cubes from the bag and place each by its class's declared side, for or against.
   3. Everyone secretly commits Influence, then reveals together; each `1` Influence counts as 1 additional vote.
   4. If the yes votes are **at least** equal to the no votes, the Bill passes: move the policy, the proposer gains `3` VP; other supporters who actually contributed at least 1 vote gain `1` VP. If it fails, nobody scores.
   5. Discard the winning side's cubes and spent Influence; return the losing side's cubes to the bag. Then immediately resolve the changes caused by the new policy.

   ![A policy proposal becomes law only after the parliamentary vote resolves](/images/rules/hegemony/policy-vote.svg)

   > If everyone is on the same side, still draw 5 cubes; they are all discarded. If the bag runs out, refill it twice before drawing again; the State does not gain extra Influence from this.


8. <!-- rule-item: seven-policies -->
   **The Seven Policies**


   ![Official politics table: seven policies and their A / B / C positions](/images/rules/hegemony/official-politics-table.jpg)

   | Policy | A | B | C |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | **Fiscal Policy** | 9 public companies; State loan limit 2 | 6 companies; limit 2 | 3 companies; limit 1 |
   | **Labour Market** | Only wage level 3 allowed | Wage levels 2–3 allowed | Wage levels 1–3 allowed |
   | **Taxation** | Base multiplier 3, welfare modifiers doubled | Base 2, normal modifiers | Base 1, ignore welfare modifiers |
   | **Health & Welfare** | Public Health free; multiplier +2 | 5 each; multiplier +1 | 10 each; no modifier |
   | **Education** | Public Education free; multiplier +2 | 5 each; multiplier +1 | 10 each; no modifier |
   | **Foreign Trade** | Food / Luxury tariff 10 / 6; no Business Deals | Tariff 5 / 3; 1 Deal | No tariff; 2 Deals |
   | **Immigration** | No extra immigrants | 1 immigration card per class | 2 immigration cards per class |

   When Fiscal Policy moves toward a larger public sector, the State must pay to add public companies; if the unemployment area holds enough matching workers to fill a new company, place them immediately and commit them. When the sector shrinks, the relevant public companies are flipped and sold and their workers return to unemployment. When a policy moves, immediately update illegal wages, Health and Education prices, tariffs, and the tax multiplier.


9. <!-- rule-item: scoring-phase -->
   **Scoring**


   After Elections, each class scores the current round. Apply unresolved State Event penalties first; after round 5, resolve loans before the end-game bonuses.

   ---


<!-- rule-section: class-play -->
## How Each Class Plays

<!-- rule-ui: sidebar default=working-class -->
1. <!-- rule-item: working-class -->
   **The Working Class**


   ![Official Working Class board and action reference](/images/rules/hegemony/official-working-class.jpg)

   | Timing | What you do |
   |---|---|
   | **Preparation** | Pay `5` interest per Loan and lose `1` Prosperity (minimum 0, no VP penalty); draw 5 up to 7; gain 2 unskilled workers; reveal 0 / 1 / 2 immigration cards if the Immigration policy allows |
   | **Basic Actions** | **Assign workers** (up to 3) · **form a Trade Union** · **buy goods / services** · **strike** · **demonstrate** · **apply pressure** |
   | **Free Actions** | Spend Health / Education / Luxury equal to Population to raise Prosperity `+1` and score the new space immediately; spend Health to gain `2` VP and 1 unskilled worker; spend Education to upgrade or retrain 1 worker. Also: swap workers, claim State benefits (4-player only), or pay `50` to repay a Loan |
   | **Resolution** | Collect wages from other players' companies for your workers; each Cooperative Farm produces 2 Food; each Union gives 1 Influence. Income tax uses the "Labour Market × Taxation" per-Population figure |
   | **Scoring** | `2` VP per Union. End game: each A in policies 1–5 adds up by `4 / 8 / 12 / 18`; every `10` money left gives 1 VP, max 15 |

   - **Assign workers**: a regular company must be completely staffed or empty; skilled slots only accept the matching skilled worker. Newly assigned workers lie down to show they are committed.
   - **Form a Trade Union**: you first need at least 4 **other** workers in that industry, then place one skilled worker into the matching Union. Union workers stay lying down and earn no wages; if the industry drops below 4 workers, the Union immediately disbands.
   - **Buy goods**: pick one resource and buy from up to two sources; each source sells up to your Population in total.
   - **Strike**: place strike markers on up to 2 companies that employ your workers. You cannot target companies with committed workers or wage level 3. If a struck company would still operate at wage level 1 / 2, it skips production and wages, and you gain 1 Influence.
   - **Demonstrate**: requires unemployment at least 2 higher than available worker slots. If the demonstration is not resolved by production: you gain 1 Influence, and the other players together lose VP equal to "unemployed workers + Unions" (capped at their empty company slots).
   - **Apply pressure**: add 3 of your cubes to the voting bag.


2. <!-- rule-item: capitalist-class -->
   **The Capitalist Class**


   ![Official Capitalist Class board and action reference](/images/rules/hegemony/official-capitalist-class.jpg)

   | Timing | What you do |
   |---|---|
   | **Preparation** | Pay `5` per Loan; draw 5 up to 7; you may discard and refill the company market to 4; refill Business Deals and swap export cards per Foreign Trade |
   | **Basic Actions** | Build a company with Revenue (up to 12; may immediately hire and commit from unemployment) · sell a company (not with committed workers) · sell abroad per export card · buy Food / Luxury cheaply with a Business Deal · spend `30` Capital for 3 Influence · add 3 cubes to the bag |
   | **Free Actions** | Change price or wage (raising wages commits the workers, and committed workers cannot be lowered) · spend `5` to pay a bonus to an operating company and commit them · spend `20` to expand storage for one resource · claim benefits (4-player only) · pay `50` Capital to repay a Loan |
   | **Resolution** | Each operating company pays one wage and produces. Pay employment tax "operating companies × tax multiplier" first, then corporate tax on the remaining Revenue |
   | **Scoring** | Move all Revenue into Capital and score by the Capital wealth space; the wealth marker gains `3` VP per space it moves right. End game: `4 / 8 / 12 / 18` per C policy, plus stored-resource icons; `-5` VP per unpaid Loan |

   > **Revenue** has not been taxed yet; **Capital** already has. Usually spend Revenue first, then Capital; when mandatory payments exceed both, take a Loan and put the `50` in the Capital area.

   Standard storage: Food max 8, other resources max 12. Excess Food and Luxury may go to the free-trade area. Traded resources in normal storage pay tariffs and can be sold domestically; in the free-trade area they pay no tariff but can only be exported later.


3. <!-- rule-item: middle-class -->
   **The Middle Class**


   ![Official Middle Class board and action reference](/images/rules/hegemony/official-middle-class.jpg)

   | Timing | What you do |
   |---|---|
   | **Preparation** | Pay `5` per Loan and lose `2` Prosperity (minimum 0); draw 5 up to 7; tidy the company market and refill to 3; gain 1 unskilled worker and 1 skilled worker of your choice; the Immigration policy may add more |
   | **Basic Actions** | Propose a Bill · assign workers · build / sell companies · export · buy goods and services · **Extra Shift** · apply pressure |
   | **Free Actions** | Use Health / Education / Luxury · change price or wage · swap workers · claim benefits · repay a Loan |
   | **Resolution** | Your own companies employing only Middle workers pay no wage; companies with Working-Class employee slots pay that wage and gain the printed employee-output bonus. First collect your workers' wages from other companies, then eat per Population |
   | **Scoring** | Prosperity scores immediately; if Prosperity is lower than your fully staffed operating companies, gain a free Prosperity `+1` and score the new space. End game: `3 / 6 / 10 / 15` per B policy, plus stored icons; every `15` money left gives 1 VP |

   - Your own companies only take Middle workers and are still fully staffed or empty.
   - **Extra Shift**: one operating company produces once more right away — pay its wage again and commit its workers.
   - Income tax looks at "Labour Market × tax table × Middle workers in non-owned companies"; employment tax is "operating companies × tax multiplier".
   - A company with Working-Class employee slots only counts as staffed once those slots are really filled.


4. <!-- rule-item: state-class -->
   **The State**


   ![Official State board and action reference](/images/rules/hegemony/official-state.jpg)

   | Timing | What you do |
   |---|---|
   | **Preparation** | Pay `5` per Loan; reveal 2 Events and 1 public Agenda; draw 5 up to 7 |
   | **Basic Actions** | Propose a Bill · address an Event · export · meet party representatives (give another player 2 Personal Influence, gain `+1` Legitimacy with them) · Extra Tax (every other player gives you `10`; your two lowest Legitimacy tracks each `-1`) · campaign (convert up to 3 Media Influence into Personal Influence) |
   | **Resolution** | Public companies pay wages and produce; other players may buy Food from the State. When refilling the voting bag, add no cubes; gain Personal Influence equal to your lowest Legitimacy, and if you have any Personal Influence you must vote |
   | **Scoring** | First apply unresolved Event penalties, then score "the sum of your two lowest Legitimacy tracks"; halve all three tracks upward, then add the Legitimacy markers. Each public Agenda item that matches the current policy gives `1` VP. End game: every `30` money left in the Treasury, plus resource / media icons |

   > Personal Influence on the State board is only for effects and voting; the Media Influence other players buy sits in the public-services area. The two kinds of Influence never convert into each other.

   **Legitimacy**: the State has one track for the Working Class, the Middle Class, and the Capitalist Class. Helping a class usually raises its track; hurting it lowers it. Every time a class gets 3 free Health or Education, the State gains `1` Legitimacy with it. Legitimacy markers add a permanent `+1` modifier to every future round's scoring.

   Addressing an Event requires completing everything the card demands. When an Event asks you to "provide" resources to a class, you may buy them from other players or abroad and place them in the matching State benefit area; when someone claims that benefit, the State gains `1` VP.

<!-- rule-section: loans-imf -->
## Loans and the IMF

> **Loans rescue emergencies only — never hoard money.** You may take a Loan (gaining `50`) only when a **mandatory** payment — wages, taxes, food, or a forced charge by another player — cannot be covered. Optional actions can never borrow ahead.

- Each Preparation, pay `5` interest per Loan; usually a Free Action pays `50` to repay one.
- End game: the Capitalist loses `5` VP per unpaid Loan; other classes may pay `55` each to clear one, and every unpaid `5` money costs `1` VP.

The State checks the IMF during production. When its loans exceed the Fiscal Policy limit and it cannot pay `55` per loan, resolve in this order:

1. Discard all pending Bills (gain 1 Influence for each of your own Bills discarded).
2. Move the policies as the IMF requires, and immediately resolve the changes to public companies, wages, taxes, and import prices.
3. Repay State loans as far as possible at `55` each.
4. Reduce all three Legitimacy tracks to half their current value, rounded up.

Even if the IMF changes the Labour Market, this round's Working and Middle income tax still uses the position at which they received their wages.

<!-- rule-section: endgame -->
## Game End and Victory

As soon as round 5's scoring finishes, resolve all loans first, then each class's end-game bonus. The player with the most VP wins.

**Tie-break:** first count how often each tied player stands in their preferred column of policies 1–5 (Working looks at A, Middle at B, Capitalist at C, State at its public Agenda). If the State is among the tied players, the State wins; otherwise compare Voting cubes; if still tied, the victory is shared.

<!-- rule-section: scoring -->
### Scoring Summary

| Class | Each round | End game |
|---|---|---|
| **Working** | `2` VP per Union; Prosperity scores immediately when Health / Education / Luxury are spent, at the new space | A policies add `4 / 8 / 12 / 18`; every `10` money gives 1 VP, max 15 |
| **Capitalist** | Move Revenue to Capital, score by the wealth table; the wealth marker gains extra `3` VP per space right | C policies add `4 / 8 / 12 / 18`; stored-resource icons; `-5` VP per Loan |
| **Middle** | Prosperity scores immediately; free `+1` Prosperity if lower than fully staffed operating companies | B policies add `3 / 6 / 10 / 15`; stored-resource icons; every `15` money gives 1 VP |
| **State** | Unresolved Events penalize first; sum of the two lowest Legitimacy tracks; `1` VP per matching public Agenda item | Every `30` money in the Treasury gives 1 VP, plus the resource / media icons on the player board |

The Capitalist's per-round scoring order: pay wages and taxes → move all money from Revenue into Capital → score by the Capital wealth space → if the wealth marker moved right, gain `3` VP per space. Later, if Capital shrinks, the wealth marker never retreats and no VP is taken back.

<!-- rule-section: mistakes -->
## Common Mistakes

- A company pays the card's total wage **once**, not per worker; but a regular company must still be fully staffed to produce.
- Once committed (lying down), workers cannot be moved, have their wages lowered, or have their company sold; they stand up only after production.
- Food must be eaten. Imported goods' money returns to the supply areas; only **tariffs** go to the Treasury.
- In an election, ties pass; only supporters who actually contributed a cube or Influence gain `1` VP.
- An immediate vote resolves only that one policy; the Business Deal count from Foreign Trade updates only at the next Preparation.

<!-- rule-section: components-quick -->
## Companies and Components Quick Reference

Companies come in five industries: Agriculture (green), Luxury (blue), Health (red), Education (orange), and Media (purple). The card shows the build cost, required workers, three wage levels, output, and possible machine bonuses. Middle-Class companies come in two kinds: those employing only Middle workers, and those that may also employ Working-Class workers.

- An **fully automatic company** has no worker slots and a large machine illustration; it always counts as operating, pays no production wage, and counts as having a machine marker.
- A regular company's **machine marker** is not automation. It still will not produce without workers; a company holds at most 1 machine marker, and only if the card shows a machine icon.
- **Cooperative Farms** are private companies the Working Class builds beside its player board with cards; they do not go on the main board. Place 3 unskilled workers; they produce 2 Food. These workers count toward the industry's worker count and can help reach the Union threshold.
- Standard storage spaces are bought by the Capitalist or obtained through Middle-Class cards and hold double. The Capitalist's **free-trade area** holds only Food and Luxury, and goods placed there can only be exported, never sold to other players.

The base game has about 270 regular cards, plus Business Deal, immigration, and Loan cards, the main board, 4 player boards, and various markers. For exact card wording, see the official rulebook linked at the end of this page.

### Income Tax per Population

| Labour Market \ Taxation | 3A | 3B | 3C |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 2A | 7 | 6 | 5 |
| 2B | 4 | 4 | 4 |
| 2C | 1 | 2 | 3 |

The Middle Class also pays **employment tax**: operating companies × the current tax multiplier. The Capitalist pays the same way; staffed or fully automatic companies count as operating, regular unstaffed ones do not.

<!-- rule-section: corporate-tax -->
### Capitalist Corporate Tax

Pay employment tax first, then read the Revenue remaining:

| Revenue | Tax A | Tax B | Tax C |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 5–9 | 1 | 2 | 2 |
| 10–24 | 5 | 5 | 4 |
| 25–49 | 12 | 10 | 7 |
| 50–99 | 24 | 15 | 10 |
| 100–199 | 40 | 30 | 20 |
| 200–299 | 100 | 70 | 40 |
| 300+ | 160 | 120 | 60 |

Read the tax multiplier together with policies 3, 4, and 5: Tax A starts at 3 and doubles Health / Education modifiers; Tax B starts at 2 and adds modifiers normally; Tax C starts at 1 and ignores both modifiers entirely. Health and Education add `+2` / `+1` / `+0` when in position A / B / C.

<!-- rule-section: official-rulings -->
## Official Rulings

![Official rules FAQ: action cards, events, and edge cases](/images/rules/hegemony/official-rules-faq.jpg)

<!-- rule-section: companies-workers-strikes -->
### Companies, Workers, and Strikes

- As long as a struck company's workers are not committed, the Working Class may still move them away and the company may still be sold. If a struck company is no longer operating at resolution, only remove the marker; the workers gain no Influence.
- When a Middle-Class company lacks its Middle workers, the Working Class cannot count its employee slots toward the demonstration's available slots.
- If one action moves a worker out and another in, a non-Middle company may not hold both committed and uncommitted workers at once; if it happens, treat all of them as uncommitted. "Assign workers" alone cannot commit workers already in a company.
- When the public sector shrinks via Fiscal Policy, a public company with committed workers may be sold; this is the exception to "a company with committed workers cannot be sold".
- A forced wage increase from a policy change does not commit workers; only a Free-Action "change wage" increase does.
- Prosperity can still be gained at the maximum; the marker does not move, but you score the top space's VP.

<!-- rule-section: loans-storage-imf -->
### Loans, Storage, and the IMF

- Selling resources to the State cannot exceed its storage capacity; if capacity is fine but the State has no money, the State must take a Loan to pay. With 2 players, Luxury sold to the State by the Capitalist returns to the supply.
- When the IMF closes public companies and lowers a storage limit, excess resources are immediately discarded; if a Middle-Class public company closes, the Middle Class no longer pays employment tax for it this round's tax step.
- The State cannot perform "Extra Tax" or a card effect that lowers Legitimacy if the corresponding track is already at 1.

<!-- rule-section: action-cards-events -->
### Action Cards and Events

- When a card tells you to "assign workers", the usual action rules still apply: the assigned workers become committed.
- **A Matter of High Priority / Shift Focus**: unchosen Events return to the top of the Event deck; with two Events in play, address either one. In round 1, shuffle back and redraw any Event that only applies in rounds 2–5.
- **Cooperative Farm**: workers placed when building it are committed that round; later they may leave, the farm stays beside the board and can be restaffed. Farm workers count toward the Agriculture Union's threshold of 4.
- **Emergency State** changes a policy without VP; only policies passed by election give VP. **Extra Shift** cannot be played if you cannot pay the wage.
- **Healthcare Benefits / State Scholarship** halve the total price, rounded up; even when the public price is already 5, the State still gains the VP from the benefit tile.
- **Need for Change / Push Political Agenda** have two separate Bills; each immediate vote costs 1 Influence, and you cannot propose two Bills for the same policy.
- **Privatization**: adjust the total number of public companies when Fiscal Policy changes afterwards; when the card sells a public company with committed workers, the workers stay where they are and keep receiving wages as agreed. When the Capitalist later sells that company card, it returns to the public area face down.
- **Civil Unrest**: the State uses one of its own Bill markers to propose the policy another player chooses; that player must choose if able, and the State decides whether to vote immediately and whose Influence is spent.
- **Infant Industry Requires Help**: the State chooses which company to build from the market; the company's owner decides whether and how to fill optional workers. A Middle-Class company cannot be built if the required Middle workers are not in unemployment.
- Personal and Media Influence never convert and cannot be bought temporarily to "provide"; only the State's campaign action converts Media Influence into Personal Influence.
