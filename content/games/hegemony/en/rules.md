# Hegemony: Lead Your Class to Victory Rules

## Overview

In **Hegemony: Lead Your Class to Victory**, 2–4 players run the Working Class, Middle Class, Capitalist Class, or the State in one fictional modern country. The four class decks play very differently, but workers, companies, money, taxes, public services, and policy votes link every player’s choices.

## Game Objective

Lead your assigned class to prosperity and political influence over five rounds. Gain **victory points** from the opportunities and goals available to your class; after final scoring, the player with the most VP wins.

## Components

- Main board: policy tracks, Parliament, election track, markets, banks, and shared supply areas
- Four asymmetric class player boards, class decks, starting cards, markers, money, influence, and workers
- Company cards and company / worker pieces for the private economy
- Policy cards, agenda cards, event cards, election materials, and round / first-player markers
- Player aids and the class-specific rulebooks

## Setup

1. Choose the class roles appropriate for the player count; use the official setup for unused classes and neutral components.
2. Place the main board, policy markers, market supplies, election materials, and round marker as directed by the main rulebook.
3. Each player takes the board, deck, starting cards, money, workers, influence, and pieces for their class. Set up starting companies, workers, and public services exactly as their class setup specifies.
4. Prepare the visible company market and policy / event decks. Give the first-player marker to the player specified by the setup.
5. Each player reviews their **class objective and action cards** before beginning. Class-specific rules and card text take priority over this overview.

## How to Play

### Round loop: take actions → resolve production → run elections when scheduled → refresh

The game lasts **five rounds**. In each action phase, players take turns in order, normally playing a card from hand to take its action or using one of the general actions available to their class. Continue until every player has passed; a player who passes normally takes no further actions that phase.

![Each class uses its own action cards, but all actions feed one shared economy](/images/rules/hegemony/shared-economy.svg)

### The four classes

| Class | What it primarily manages |
|---|---|
| **Working Class** | Workers, employment, unions, consumption, and social reforms |
| **Middle Class** | Its household and smaller businesses; it both employs and works |
| **Capitalist Class** | Companies, investment, production, wages, sales, and profits |
| **State** | Public services, taxation, subsidies, public debt, and political balance |

Your cards state the precise costs, limits, and timing of your class’s actions. A strong move often needs another class to cooperate indirectly: companies need workers and buyers; households need jobs and income; the State needs taxes to fund services.

### Companies, work, and consumption

Companies enter the economy through the relevant class actions. They need appropriate workers before they can produce. During production, eligible companies pay wages, make goods or services available, and can generate income for their owner; households spend income on consumption as permitted. Follow the order and exceptions on the board, cards, and class aids—many effects depend on whether a worker is employed, a company is staffed, or a good is available.

### Policies and influence

Policies shape the shared rules: taxation, labour arrangements, welfare, trade, and other areas can change the incentives of every class. Players gain and spend **influence** to propose, support, or oppose policy changes. Policy cards and the current policy position determine what is legal and how much an action costs or pays.

![A policy proposal becomes law only after the parliamentary vote resolves](/images/rules/hegemony/policy-vote.svg)

### Elections

At the rounds specified on the election track, pause for an **election**. Resolve campaigning and vote effects in the required order, then adjust political representation and any resulting policy opportunities. Elections are not a separate private minigame: economic results, class support, and the cards played beforehand can all affect them.

### Production and round end

After the action phase, complete the production / economy procedure in the printed order. Resolve company production, wages, consumption, taxes, public payments, debt, and card effects that trigger in that phase. Then perform the round-end refresh: discard or retain cards as instructed, refill public displays, ready components, advance the round marker, and update turn order when required.

## Game End and Scoring

After the fifth round’s final procedures, resolve end-game scoring using the main board, class boards, and objective / agenda cards. Add all VP sources applicable to your class. The highest total wins; settle a tie with the official tie-breaker.

## Special Rules

- **Asymmetry is intentional.** Do not assume that an action available to one class exists for another; use the card text and class aid.
- **Public information matters.** Track policy positions, market availability, employment, wages, taxes, and elections on the shared board.
- **Card text overrides summaries.** When a card, policy, or class rulebook conflicts with this guide, follow the official text.
- For first games, use the recommended setup and introductory guidance in the official rulebook; it establishes the economic links the game expects.
