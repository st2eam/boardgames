# Petiquette Rules

## Overview

**Petiquette** (动物礼仪师 / ペチケ) is a 2025 party game by **Thomas Sellner**, published by **Oink Games** (artwork **Jun Sasaki**, illustrations **Hisanori Hiraoka**). Ages 7+.

Humans are curious creatures. Even when cards are randomly lined up, we can't help but find patterns...and ideally we're thinking along the same lines.

Each round in Petiquette, players look at a line-up of five random cards — with cards showing one of three animals that is wearing one of three hats, with a number from 1–5 at top — and try to find some logic or pattern within them. Each player secretly chooses the card they think would most properly fit into that pattern. For example, if the sequence is "red, red, yellow...", you might feel that yellow should come next. If it's "dog, cat, bird...", then maybe dog feels right...but what about "dog, cat, dog, cat, bird"? No single answer is correct, but if someone chooses the same card as you, you both earn points.

Petiquette can be played either competitively or co-operatively.

**Players:** 2–6 · **Time:** about 20 minutes.

## Components

- **45 sequence cards** — every mix of **3 animals** (dog, cat, bird), **3 hat colours** (red, yellow, blue), and **numbers 1–5**
- **1 question (?) card**
- **6 player sets** of **9 pattern cards** each (every animal + hat pair) plus a **clip-on number wheel** (1–5)
- **Round tokens** and **score chips** (47 chips in the box)
- Rulebook (Japanese / English; Chinese editions include a Chinese booklet)

## Setup

1. Give each player a **pattern-card set** and a **number wheel**.
2. Put **round tokens** and **score chips** beside the table.
3. Shuffle the **45** sequence cards.
4. Draw **five**, shuffle the **?** in with them, and lay all **six** face up in a row. The **?** can sit at either end or in the middle.

## How to Play (competitive)

![Five cards plus a ? anywhere in the row; invent what belongs in the gap](/images/rules/petiquette/sequence.svg)

A game is **five rounds**. Each round:

1. Look at the six-card **sequence**. Treat the **?** as a hole you will fill.
2. Secretly build an answer: pick one **pattern card** (animal + hat) and clip on a **number**. Play it face down.
3. When everyone is ready, **reveal together**.
4. Talk through why you chose that fill — that discussion is the point of the game.
5. Score, discard the five sequence cards, keep the **?**, draw five new cards, shuffle the **?** in, and lay a new row.

![Clip a 1–5 wheel onto a pattern card; do not copy a combo already in the row](/images/rules/petiquette/compose.svg)

### Legal answers

- You may pick **any** animal + hat + number you like **except** a combination that **already appears** as a face-up sequence card this round.
- The **?** itself is not a combination; it only marks the hole.

### Scoring

![Same dog + red hat + 3 as someone else: both take a chip. A unique answer scores nothing](/images/rules/petiquette/match-score.svg)

If **at least one other player** built the **exact** same animal + hat + number as you, you score:

| Rounds | Points if you matched anyone |
|--------|------------------------------|
| 1–3 | **1** |
| 4–5 | **2** |

Matching extra people does **not** add extra points — either you matched or you didn’t.

Players who chose a unique combination score **0** this round.

## Game End

After round 5, the player with the **most chips** wins. Ties share the win.

## Cooperative mode

One player is the **Lead**. They secretly compose the card they think belongs in the **?**. Everyone else **discusses** and tries to guess that exact combination (as a group, or by agreeing on one guess).

Rotate the Lead each round. The included goal is to **match all five rounds** in a row. If a round misses, the team can start a new streak or just keep playing for the conversation.

## Tips

- There is no “correct” sequence. Obvious hat colours or simple animal cycles are often easier to match than clever number arithmetic.
- Listen to why people scored last round — groups quickly share a house style.
- If the row feels too readable, the next shuffle of five + **?** usually breaks it.
